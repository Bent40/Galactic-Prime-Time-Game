## 1. The chain

```
 A PLAGUE DEMON rises on the strength of its disease.
            │
 It KILLS A DRAGON with that disease ──────▶ the deed that makes its name.
            │
 The plague does not stop. It spreads.
 It kills EVERY ONE OF ITS OWN FOLLOWERS.
            │
            ▼
 ZERO REVERENCE. Its weapon ate its congregation.  ◀── the bankruptcy
            │
 Desperate to cure them, it turns to DRAGON'S BLOOD — and finds a method.
            │
 An OPPOSING GOD schemes: sends CHAMPIONS to suppress it,
 and it is defeated before it can execute the cure.
            │
            ▼
 Buried. Trapped underground. Mute.
```

**Then the clock runs, and the campaign's existing time skips become the arc:**

## 2. The floor-runner is buried under its own floor

The F1–F3 set's bankrupt god (Q-29) **is the plague demon** — and it is *underneath the
players for three floors*.

Its bankruptcy is the cleanest possible statement of Q-14. Divinity is named, living beings
who revere you; **its plague killed every one of them.** It did not lose its followers'
faith — it lost their *lives*, and under the rules that is the same thing. **Its greatest
weapon deleted its own income.**

No villainy is needed to explain it, which is exactly the morally-alien register canon
requires (`../cosmic-casino-canon.md` §4). And its debt to the house (Q-29) is now
legible: it must gather enough reverence to buy back its right to exist, from inside a
grave, with no tongue to ask.

## 3. The myth-source relic — the demon's core

Q-33 requires each floor-set to hide the source of its god's myth. Here it is **a body part**
(owner's ruling), found beneath the palace on F3 or on the Easy route at F1.

**Recommendation: the tongue.** ⟨proposal — **Q-41**⟩

| It has to do | The tongue does it |
|---|---|
| Gate **communication** (Q-33) | A god with no tongue cannot answer. Recovering it is *literally* opening the channel |
| Carry the **naming** economy (Q-14) | The tongue is the organ of naming and of prayer — the two verbs the whole economy runs on |
| Be a plague **vector** | Disease carried on breath and speech. Its weapon and its voice are the same organ |
| Reward **ransacking** | Taken rather than restored, it should grant plague-speech — power that talks, from a god who no longer can |

Alternatives the owner floated: poison-spewing gloves/hands (stronger in combat, weaker on
the communication gate), or the heart. The tongue is the only one that makes *finding it* and
*talking to it* the same act.

## 4. The choice at the grave

Both routes converge on one decision — Q-29's save-or-condemn, made concrete:

| | **Pray for its revival** | **Ransack the grave and take its power** |
|---|---|---|
| You give | Reverence you cannot get back (Q-22: earned only) | Nothing |
| You get | A god that owes you; demons released from their appetites | Its power, immediately |
| You lose | Divinity you could have spent or held (Q-05) | **Every demon becomes your enemy** |
| Scores | *"Cure what's corrupted"* — at your own cost | *"…and at what cost?"* — answered by someone else paying |

This is the set's question with the price tag showing, and it lands on the **verdict axes**
(`../story-canon.md:78-82`) without needing a bespoke instrument.

---

## 5. Dragon's blood does two things

1. **It cures the crystallization disease.**
2. **It soothes the appetite of demons** — which is why reviving the demon god releases
   demons from their appetites: it finishes the cure it was suppressed before completing.

**The dragon learns this** — from the demons and from **Nullrot**, the demon god's champion.
Under **Q-39** the Loong is the **descendant** of the dragon the plague demon killed.

> **This is what fixes the Loong.** `../review/` flagged its escort-quest weakness as
> something the frame could not repair. It still can't repair the quest *shape* — but the
> Loong is no longer cargo. It is the **only cure in the world**, hunted for its blood, with
> a blood claim against the thing buried under the capital, learning all of this from its
> ancestor's killer's champion. It now wants things, and things want it.
